Bias is a four-letter word

Imagine this:
1.	You are Black
2.	You are a woman
3.	You are an immigrant from a stigmatized country

... students burst into laughter and exchange glances with one another, when you are trying to explain something important, in all seriousness, to the class.

...  a student aggressively shoves their completed exam into your open hand. Their answer to your smiling face and “are you finished?” is only “Here!”.

...  a pair of students comes to your office to take issue with incorrect responses on their exam. Being disappointed with the outcome, one abruptly walks away declaring that she is 'leaving before ...(doing something)... because you are making her angry'.

...  you, in an effort to seem relatable, tell a student (who is wearing an Arsenal Football Club jersey) that your family follows Arsenal. They ask to give you a High-5. You accept; but they hit you so hard that your hand stings for a prolonged period of time. It is clear that they intentionally hurt you.

...  you enjoy teaching and devote much time to course preparation. You take care that your syllabus, LMS course site, assessments, and rubrics are well presented and thoughtfully aligned with course learning objectives. You’ve been teaching at the College Level for over 20 years. Yet, semester after semester, your course evaluations include comments like: “Horrible, horrible, horrible” and “Do not teach this class again”.

...  After taking his final exam, a white male student twice your size stands extremely close with his back turned to the class. Looking down at you, he begins complaining about his grade and demands extra-credit. You step aside –to escape from him, to restore your personal space, and to continue observing the class you are proctoring. He persists – defiantly standing closer than before and again obstructing your view, until other students approach the two of you to return their exams. You wonder if he might be waiting for you outside the classroom and could hurt you.


Now that you have imagined these things (all of which happened to me), what would you say are the chances that anonymous student evaluations of teaching (SETs) for minoritized faculty are sometimes biased, harassing, or intimidating?
Twice a year, course evaluations that are full of unfounded, fabricated, and hurtful remarks push me into a state of despair. Feeling helpless and hopeless, I wonder ‘what I have done to deserve this’ as I await the inevitable reprimand from people with power to hire/fire.  This year, I am fighting back the one way I know how: with data. I compiled resources and designed a “SET Bias Assessment Tool” to help other weary educators highlight evidence of discrimination in their course evaluations. As information, student feedback can be helpful. It should not be used as a weapon. When it harms, let’s call it what it is: BIAS, a four-letter word.
