A workflow for flagging SET bias

The goal is not to replace judgment. The goal is to surface patterns that deserve careful human review before evaluation data is used in high-stakes personnel decisions.

Follow the full workflow
  1. Assemble the evidence package. Organize SET scores, comments, course materials, peer reviews, grade distributions, and relevant context.
  2. Use AI as a pattern-flagging aid. Prompt the tool to identify likely bias categories, credibility gaps, grade-retaliation patterns, and defamatory content.
  3. Review findings with qualified humans. Use the output as documentation for administrators, equity reviewers, faculty advocates, or institutional review processes.

Bias categories at a glance

The reference table organizes SET bias into four primary types plus compound categories that can appear when multiple bias mechanisms operate together.

Type 1 — Direct Identity Bias Explicit or coded language about authority, competence, accent, temperament, race, gender, or belonging.
Type 2 — Proxy Bias Pedagogical complaints that may mask differential expectations around grading, rigor, clarity, or responsiveness.
Type 3 — Stigmatized Identity Bias Patterns tied to identities that carry specific social stigma, political targeting, or “forever foreigner” framing.
Type 4 — Deliberate Defamation Harassment, fabricated accusations, coordinated attacks, grade retaliation, or reputational harm.
Compound Categories Overlapping patterns that combine identity, pedagogy, course content, institutional context, and power.
!
Important use note: This site is a decision-support resource. AI can help flag patterns and organize evidence, but final determinations should be made by qualified human reviewers with knowledge of the research literature, institutional context, and specific case materials.
